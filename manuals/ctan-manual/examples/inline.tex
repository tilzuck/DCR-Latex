The relation $A \dcrresponse B$ makes $B$ pending if $A$ is executed,
while $A \dcrcondition B$ expresses that $A$ must have been executed
before $B$ can be executed. Both of these relations can be timed, by
using the \texttt{delay} or \texttt{deadline} option.
$A \dcrcondition[delay=5d] B$ expresses that $A$ must have been executed
5 days before $B$ can be executed, and $A \dcrresponse[deadline=5d] B$
states that $B$ must be executed within 5 days.

The other available inline relations are $A \dcrinclude B$ and
$A \dcrexclude B$ ($A$ includes or excludes $B$), $A \dcrmilestone B$
($B$ cannot be executed while $A$ is pending), $A \dcrprecondition B$
(a condition and a milestone in one), $A \dcrlogicalinclude B$,
$A \dcrupdate B$ ($A$ updates the value of $B$), $A \dcrnoresponse B$
($A$ removes the pending of $B$) and $A \dcrspawn B$ ($A$ spawns $B$).

Every relation can have a guard, written above the line with the
\texttt{guard} option: $A \dcrinclude[guard={x > 5}] B$ includes $B$
only if $x > 5$. Guard and time can be combined:
$A \dcrresponse[guard=ok, deadline=5d] B$.
